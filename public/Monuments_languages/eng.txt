1. Shri Jagannath Temple and Subsidiary Shrines, Puri
Location: Puri, Puri District
Period: 12th Century C.E.
Coordinates: Lat. 19°48'20"N, Long. 85°49'05"E
Protected Area Status: S.O. 2111 (July 12, 1974); S.O. 560 (February 3, 1975)
Description: The famous temple of Lord Jagannath, Puri is located on the sea shore of Bay of Bengal in Puri. The most stupendous architectural edifice of the Ganga period was the construction of the Jagannatha temple at Puri. On the basis of Dasgopa Copper Plate inscription of the Ganga monarch Rajaraja III, issued in the year 1198 C.E., the temple of Lord Jagannatha was built by Chodagangadeva of the Ganga dynasty during circa second half of the 11th century C.E. The construction of the main temple is said to be completed by the grandson of Chodagangadeva, King Anangabhimadeva I. The constructions continued for centuries afterwards. The inner enclosure wall and bhogamandapa are credited to Purushottama Deva who ruled in the 15th century C.E. It is believed that there could have been an earlier temple because the place was already a popular pilgrimage of Lord Purushottam. Puranas mention Puri as Nilachala, Purushottama Kshetra, Shree Kshetra etc.

The temple complex has two enclosure walls; the exterior one is named as Meghanada prachira while the interior one as Kurmibedha. Facing east, the main temple consists of deul, jagamohana, natamandira and bhogamandapa, all placed in the same axial alignment. The shikhara of the deul rises to a height of upto 65 mtrs dominating the whole surrounding within the inner enclosure where there are many smaller shrines dedicated to Narasimha, Surya, Vimala, Lakshmi, Ganesha and others.

 The temple has four gateways. The main one, on the east is called Simhadvara (Lion gate) while those on the south, west and north are known as Ashvadvara (Horse gate), Vyaghradvara (Tiger gate) and Hastidvara (Elephant gate) respectively. In front of the simhadvara is installed the Aruna-stambha, brought from the Sun temple, Konark by the Marathas in circa 18th century C.E.

The deul is pancharatha on plan and is noted for its heights and stupendous form. The carvings on the exterior walls of the main temple, hitherto concealed under a thick coat of lime plaster, have been de-plastered in the recent past and repaired under a scheme of elaborate conservation brought to light fine sculptures of various forms of Vishnu, Dikpalas and other Brahmanical divinities, besides decorative naga-nagis, vyalas, etc.
Standing on an ornamental platform, the bada of the deul is panchangbada in elevation as in a full-fledged Odishan temple. It has five-fold vertical divisions such as pabhaga, lower jangha, bandhana, upper jangha and varanda.

The pabhaga consists of five mouldings namely khura, kumbha, pata, kani and basanata. The mouldings are connected by vertical bands, relieved with scroll-work and figures of kanyas. The khuras have niches in the central facet capped by vajramundis. The kani is plain but pata and basanta are richly carved. The sculptured friezes of the pata mostly depict episodes from the life of Krishna. Noteworthy among these are Sakatabhanga, Kaliyadalana, lifting of Govardhana, fighting with Kesin, Kubalaya, Aritsha, etc.
The lower jangha has khakhara-mundis in the kanika and anuratha. The corner khakhara-mundis contain seated figures of eight dikpalas while those of anuratha have seated kanyas. The upper jangha has pidha-mundis containing deities including avataras of Vishnu such as Matsya, Kurma, Varaha, Buddha, Kalki, etc. The three rahapaga contain niches for parshvadevatas namely Varaha, Trivikrama and Narasimha respectively in the southern, the northern and western direction. 

The jagamohana is a pyramidal temple with pidhas arranged in two tiers like that of the Lingaraj temple, but it marks an improvement over the latter in having four doors, the west leading to the garbha-griha, the east door leading to the nata-mandira, the south door opening out into the compound and the north door leading to Ratnabhandara of the temple. Inside the jagamohana of the temple, there is a fine chlorite door frame.
The nata-mandira is divided within by four rows of pillars, four pillars in each rows. The pillars are of unequal size. The bhoga-manadap stands on a richly sculptured pitha and the exterior walls are richly carved with sculptural images.

2. Athara Nala Bridge over Madhupur Stream, Puri
Location: Puri, Puri District
Period: 18th Century C.E.
Coordinates: Lat. 19°49'11"N, Long. 85°49'54"E
Protected Area Status: No. 2492E (December 1, 1914); No. 575E (March 22, 1915)
Description: The Atharanala Bridge also locally known as Hulhuliya bridge is located in Puri. This bridge with eighteen openings or the Atharanala bridge is constructed over the Madhupur stream in the town of Puri. It is measuring 88.4m in length, constructed in the mediaeval architectural scheme of pillars and arches. It is composed of nineteen spans ranging from 2.13m to 4.87m across. The building materials used are laterite and sandstone blocks. It was built by the Maratha rulers who ruled over Odisha in circa 18th century C.E. and built bridges in important places where communication was needed.


3. Varahi Temple, Chaurasi
Location: Chaurasi, Puri District
Period: 9th–10th Century C.E.
Coordinates: Lat. 20°03'31"N, Long. 86°07'07"E
Protected Area Status: S.O. 1112 (April 5, 1962); S.O. 2653 (August 14, 1962)
Description: The east facing Varahi Temple, Chaurashi, is located in Prachi valley. The temple consists of a khakhara vimana and a rectangular jagamohana having a roof of two terraces. It is a pancharatha temple having triangabada. The temple style itself suggests its vaulted roof design of mastaka.The temple is dedicated to goddess Varahi. The deity is depicted in Lalitasana with two arms and holds a fish in her right hand and a kapala in her left and her vahana buffalo. A third eye is depicted on her forehead. The vimana is rectangular on plan whereas the jagamohana is oblong on plan. The temple is lavishly decorated with sculptural motifs and scroll work. On the basis of architectural style, the temple can be dated to the 9th century C.E.


4. Sun Temple and Ancient Ruins (Black Pagoda), Konark
Location: Konark, Puri District
Period: 13th Century C.E.
Coordinates: Lat. 19°53'15.30"N, Long. 86°05'41.05"E
Protected Area Status: No. 2493E (December 1, 1914); No. 575E (March 22, 1915)
Description: Konark, also known as Arka-kshetra or Padma-kshetra, owes its singular distinction to the Sun temple located here. The temple represents the highest watermark of the kalingan style of architecture, built by Narasimhadeva-I (C.E. 1238-64) of the Ganga dynasty. The face of the high plinth on either side is carved with twelve pairs of wheels justifying the description of the temple as a chariot. The main temple complex consists of a rekha sanctum, pyramidal jagmohana and a pillared hall popularly known as Nata-mandapa. The sanctum and the porch forming a single unified architectural scheme has been conceived as the celestial chariot of the Sun God who is believed in Hindu mythology to course across the sky in a chariot of seven horses.   The temple comprised of a sanctum with a lofty sikhara (now lost), a jagamohana or mandapa, 38m high and a detached nata-mandapa in the same axis, with subsidiary shrines; all enclosed in a compound wall. The sanctum and the nata-mandapa have lost their roof, the jagamohana alone having preserved its roof intact. The battered walls of all the constituents have a variegated texture enlivened by sculptural and ornamental profusion. The sanctum displays superb images of the Sun god in the three projections, while the roof tiers of the jagamohana harbor colossal figures of female musicians.

7. Prehistoric Site, Kuchai
Location: Kuchai (near Baripada), Mayurbhanj District
Period: Neolithic Period
Coordinates: Lat. 22°04'12"N, Long. 86°38'59"E
Protected Area Status: Act No. 3 of 1954 (January 2, 1954)
Description: The Pre-historic Site-Kuchai, Baripada, is located on the east of Budhabalanga river, is one of the important Neolithic excavated site in eastern India. It was excavated by B. K. Thapar of Archaeological Survey of India in 1961. The excavation revealed two fold cultural sequence of the site. The lower deposit composed of gravel mixed with loose laterite deposit yielded microliths of non-geometric industry includes blades, points, lunets, scrapers etc. the upper level yielded Neolithic artefacts in association with a coarse grit-tempered red ware, sometimes also slipped and showing incised or finger-tip decoration.


8. Prehistoric Site, Kuliana
Location: Kuliana, Mayurbhanj District
Period: Palaeolithic Period
Coordinates: Lat. 22°04'12"N, Long. 86°38'59"E
Protected Area Status: Act No. 3 of 1954 (January 2, 1954)
Description: The village of Kuliana is situated about 16 km northwest of Baripada, Mayurbhanj. The river Budhabalang flows about 1.5km west of Kuliana. It was the first excavated Paleolithic site in India in 1939 and it is a famous site for lower Paleolithic industry. A larger number of Paleolithic tools were found from a quarry and its immediate neighborhood. The industry of Kuliana was mainly a core industry comprising chopper chopping tools, handaxes, cleavers and few flake tools. The typological age of Kuliana industry is Lower Paleolithic. Quartz and quartzite are the main raw materials.



9. Fort Ruins, Haripur
Location: Haripur (Haripurgarh), Mayurbhanj District
Period: 14th–16th Century C.E.
Coordinates: Lat. 21°48'14"N, Long. 86°42'56"E
Protected Area Status: Act No. 3 of 1954 (January 2, 1954); S.O. 1540 (April 29, 1964)
Description: The Ruins of Ancient Fort, Haripur, Situated on the banks of the Budhabalanga River, about 25 km south of Baripada in the Mayurbhanj district. The site was initially the capital of the Bhanja rulers which continued till the time of Harihara Bhanja (1400 C.E.). During its heyday, the site was enclosed by a fortification wall and had many religious and secular structures. Among the surviving structural remains the Rasikaraya temple, Radhamohana temple, Jagannatha temple and excavated brick structures are noteworthy. The brick built Rasikaraya temple, dedicated to Vishnu, is in the form of athchala Bangla temple style. It is best known for its delicate relief and carvings. These brick temples are datable to 14th -16th century C.E.



10. Ravana Chhaya Rock Shelter & Monuments, Sitabhinji
Location: Sitabhinji (Danguapasi), Keonjhar District
Period: 5th–6th Century C.E.
Coordinates: Lat. 21°30'31.29"N, Long. 85°46'45.93"E
Protected Area Status: S.O. 1754 (May 16, 1964); S.O. 1915 (May 14, 1965)
Description: The rock locally known as Ravana Chhaya and other ancient monuments and remains, is located in Sitabhinji, District- Keonjhar. The paintings were executed on the ceiling of the rock shelter is namely a royal procession with a king seated on the elephant accompanied by a retinue of attendants, horsemen, footmen and a dancing girl. The painting work was drawn on polychrome of five different colours such as white, buff, brown, red and yellow ochre. There is an inscription which contains the name of Maharaja Disabhanja is painted below the ceiling.  A series of inscriptions of shell script are also found engraved on the nearby rock boulders. This is the only murals in the Eastern India contemporaneous to the Gupta period. One pancha-mukhi Sivalinga of chlorite belonging to the Gupta period is available and kept here within a grilled mandapa. On the basis of paleography records the mural can be dated to the 5th -6th century CE.

14. Chandrasekhar Temple Complex, Kapilas
Location: Kapilas Hill, Dhenkanal District
Period: 13th–14th Century C.E.
Coordinates: Lat. 20°40'54"N, Long. 85°45'48"E
Protected Area Status: S.O. 78(E) (January 15, 2004); S.O. 1358(E) (December 10, 2004)
Description: The Chandrasekhara Mahadeva temple is located on a little flat surface on the highest peak of the Kapilas hillock situated around 17km from district headquarter Dhenkanal and 4km from the village Deogaon in Gondia Tehsil.  The temple has a rekha vimana, pidha jagamohana and muktimandapa of kalingan order facing south-east dated to the 13th century C.E., built by Narashimahdeva – III, the Ganaga monarch. The temple complex beholds the shrines of Lord Viswanath, Narayan and Goddess Parvati.
The temple is decorated with architectural motifs such as- khakharamunids and pidhamundis in the talajangha and uparajangha respectively of both the vimana and jagamohana and the niches enshrined dikpalas and their consorts in the kanika pagas and nayikas in the anuratha pagas. Recesses of the pagas are adorned with gaja-vidalas, nayikas in different form such as darpana, salabhanjika, chamaradhari and royal figures etc. The other motifs are elephant rider, horse rider, birds, bharabahakas and naga-nagi pilasters in the ganthiala junction etc. Miniature rekhamundis are found on the base of the gandi on each pagas. The central raha has three rekhamundis, udyotasimha and kirtimukha. Dopichhasimhas and four forms of Siva are adorned over the beki. In the back wall raha-paga housed the image of Lord Jagannath above the udyatasimha, which makes unique about the concept of Harihara cult and association of Chandrashekhar Mahadev with Lord Jagannath. The wooden ceiling of the muktimandapa is decorated with marvellous wooden sculptures based on Krisnalila scenes, dasavatara of Visnu, gaja-vidalas and lotus design etc. The land scape of Kapilas hillock venerated by people had its beginning from time immemorial, as well as the extend remains of caves, temples, sculptures etc. show the religious activities in tangible form stating from the early historic to the late 18th century C.E.


15. Arnakotiswar (Unnakotiswar) Mahadev Temple, Latadeipur
Location: Latadeipur, Dhenkanal District
Period: 15th–16th Century C.E.
Coordinates: Lat. 20°48'33"N, Long. 85°39'31"E
Protected Area Status: S.O. 213(E) (February 17, 2004); S.O. 1359(E) (December 10, 2004)
Description: Annakotesvara temple also known as Unnakotiswar Mahadev Temple, is 22km north of the district headquarters Dhenkanal and on the right bank of river Brahmani on the outskirt of the village Latadeipur. It is a living temple of kalingan order. The temple is pancharatha on plan and panchanga bada in elevation and facing towards east. The vimana is square on plan and stands over a high platform. The temple is decorated with pidhamundis and khakharamundis in the uparajangha and talajangha respectively. These mundis are housed with different divinities, nayikas and the conjunctions in between the projection housed with gaja-vidalas, nara-vidalas. Most of the mundi niches are housing the Krisnalila scene. On the basis of architectural style and iconographical features of the sculptures, the temple can be dated to Suryavamsi Gajapati period i.e. circa 15th century C.E.


16. Rock-cut Anantasayi Vishnu, Saranga
Location: Saranga, Dhenkanal District
Period: 8th–9th Century C.E.
Coordinates: Lat. 20°55'43"N, Long. 85°14'49"E
Protected Area Status: S.O. 215(E) (February 17, 2004); S.O. 1361(E) (December 10, 2004)
Description: The colossal rock cut image of Anantasayi Vishnu is located on the left bank of river Brahamani. It is carved in high relief on an exposed rock outcrop, noteworthy for its grandeur in size. It is the largest image of its kind in reclining posture in the country.  The sculpture measures 12.07m. from head to toe. The four-armed reclining sculpture appears to be very natural and is profusely ornamented in spite of its hugeness. It is devoid of the traditional serpent hood. On the basis of iconography and crown design, such as pointed chin, prominent nose and depiction of kiritamukuta on head, this image is dated to the late part of the Bhaumakara rule i.e .9th century C.E.

19. Churanga Garh Fort (Sarangarh), Dadhapatana

Period: 11th–12th Century C.E.
Coordinates: Lat. 20°25'03"N, Long. 85°48'17"E

23. Buddhist Complex, Lalitagiri (Nalitgiri)
Location: Balichandrapur, Cuttack District
Period: 1st Century B.C.E. to 13th Century C.E.
Coordinates: Lat. 20°35'22"N, Long. 86°15'03"E
Protected Area Status: No. 570-Com. (January 25, 1937); No. 5215-IA-3/37-Com.
Description: The Ruins of Buddhist temples and images, Nalitgiri (Lalitgiri), Balichandrapur, Cuttack is located in a hillock of the Assia hill range, in the Birupa-Chitrotpala river valley. Three hills namely Olasuni, Parabhadi and Nandapahad together known as Lalitagiri is one of the earliest Buddhist site of Odisha.
A small scale excavation was conducted in 1977 by Utkal University, Bhubaneswar in the site. Extensive excavations were carried out by the Archaeological Survey of India, from 1985 to 1991, revealed cultural continuity right from the post Mauryan period to the 13th century CE and without any break. One inscribed terracotta sealing unearthed from monastery no. 4, palaeographically datable to 9th-10th century CE reads as “Sri Chandraditya vihara samagra arya bhikshu sanghasa”. The excavations also unearthed the structural remains of a Buddhist settlement includes remains of an apsidal Chaityagriha, four monasteries and a massive stupa. Two relic caskets made of khondolite stone containing steatite, silver and gold caskets recovered from the stupa is noteworthy. 
Other noteworthy antiquities are a gold ring, silver ingots, stone plaques of Ganesa, Mahisasurmardini, a seal matrix cum pendent and a tiny figure of Avalokitesvara, inscribed terracotta sealings, potsherds, dating back from post Mauryan period to 10th -11th century C.E. 32. Ratnagiri Mahavihara, Ratnagiri
Location: Ratnagiri, Jajpur District
Period: 5th–16th Century C.E.
Coordinates: Lat. 20°38'28"N to 20°38'32"N, Long. 86°20'10"E
Protected Area Status: No. 567-Com. (January 25, 1937); No. 5214-IA.3/37.Com. (June 20, 1937)
Description: The Ratnagiri Mahavihara, is situated on the Assia hill range and on the left bank of the river Kelua a tributary of river Brahmani. Buddhist remains atop the Ratnagiri or the “hill of jewels” were first noticed by M.M. Chakravarty in 1905 and subsequently excavated by Debala Mitra of the Archaeological Survey of India between 1958 and 1961 resulting in exposing a magnificent Buddhist establishment consisting of a mahastupa, monastic complexes consisting of two monasteries, cluster of shrines, votive stupas, sculptures, architectural fragments and other antiquities. 33. Udayagiri Buddhist Complex, Chandia
Location: Chandia (Udayagiri), Jajpur District
Period: 1st–13th Century C.E.
Coordinates: Lat. 20°38'30"N, Long. 86°16'09"E
Protected Area Status: No. 573-Com. (January 25, 1937); No. 5216-IA-3/37.Com.
Description: The Udayagiri Buddhist site is situated on the Assia hill range and in Chandia locality of Jajpur, on the right bank of the river Birupa. Udayagiri or the “Sun rise Hill”, a crescent shaped hill was brought to light by Babu Chandrasekhara Banurji in 1870.  The scientific excavations at the site were undertaken by the Archaeological Survey of India in two phases from 1985 to 1989 at Udayagiri-1 and again from 1997 to 2003 at Udayagiri -2.
The excavations at Udayagiri-1 has brought to light Madhavapura Mahavihara, the brick built remains of a huge monastic complex (35.0 m x 35.0 m), a 4.80m high Mahastupa of roughly square on plan with each side measuring 10m. Contains seated images of Dhyani Buddhas in cardinal directions; all inscribed with Buddhist creeds datable to 10th - 11th century C.E. The excavations at Udayagiri-2 have brought to light the remains of most impressive Simhaprastha Mahavihara, a brick built double storied monastic complex with pradakshinapatha around the sanctum, vaulted windows, existence of an upper shrine chamber on the first floor. Besides, a massive shrine complex with niches on its walls enshrining Buddhist divinities around a large inscribed image of Avalokitesvara, presumably acted as its presiding deity was also unearthed. Again, a stone platform topped by an apsidal chaityagriha, subsequently converted to a rectangular one with images of lions and a host of masonry and brick stupas, a 70m long and 1.42m wide stone paved pathway, masonry shrines and a residential-cum-kitchen complex was also exposed. Sculptures of Buddha and other Buddhist divinities like Tara, Manjusri, Bhrikuti, Hariti, Chunda, Avalokiteswara, Maitreya, Aparajita, Vairochana, Vasudhara etc. along with many antiquities of stone, terracotta and metals were recovered from the site.  The site can be assigned from 1st century C.E. to 13th century C.E.
